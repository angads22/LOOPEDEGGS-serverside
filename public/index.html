The problem

The flaw every
farmer knows.

Small-scale egg incubators share one consistent, unavoidable flaw. It has been silently reducing hatch rates for decades — and there is no commercial solution at non-factory-farm scale.

Verbatim · Founders' note

Small scale egg incubators contain one consistent flaw — they need to be regularly opened in order to candle eggs (check their growth by shining a light through the egg). This is necessary because eggs that aren't properly growing must be removed from the incubator, otherwise they risk damaging the entire batch through rot.

This process, however, exposes the eggs inside to harmful fluctuations in temperature and humidity. Egg incubation is a sensitive process, and these fluctuations can be harmful to the development of the eggs (potentially even reducing hatch rates, especially in winter months).

FIG. 02 · CHAMBER TEMPERATURE OVER A 21-DAY CYCLE SOURCE · OPEN SPACES FARMS
01

Candling requires opening

To check development, farmers must open the incubator and shine a light through every egg. Non-viable eggs must be removed immediately to prevent rot from spreading to the healthy batch.

02

Temperature spikes

Each opening causes rapid temperature swings that stress developing embryos. The carefully controlled environment is disrupted in seconds.

03

Humidity loss

Precisely maintained humidity — critical for development — drops the moment the lid lifts and takes hours to recover.

04

Worse in winter

Cold ambient air amplifies fluctuations, making winter incubation especially high-risk for small farms.

The solution

Every process now happens
inside the chamber.

We designed a prototype of an egg incubator that can automatically do all processes internally — removing the need to open the incubator. Six independent systems work together so the lid never has to open until the chicks are ready to come out.

A

Computer-vision candling

Built-in cameras analyse development on a daily schedule. No human intervention. Zero chamber exposure.

B

Automated dud removal

Non-viable eggs are identified and extracted from the batch automatically, protecting healthy eggs from contamination.

C

Per-egg tracking

Every egg is tracked individually through the cycle, giving full visibility into each embryo's progress.

D

Auto-turning

Eggs turn at optimal intervals — a critical step for preventing embryo adhesion to the shell.

E

Lockdown phase

The final days of incubation are managed automatically, with conditions optimised for hatching without intervention.

F

Companion app

Real-time updates, alerts and cycle insights — all from your phone, all the time.

Process

Three steps.
That's it.

From the moment you load the chamber to the moment the chicks arrive, you do exactly one thing — wait.

01

Load and start

Place fertilised eggs in the LifeLoop chamber and start the cycle. That is the only thing you need to do.

02

Automated monitoring

LifeLoop continuously manages temperature, humidity, turning and candling. Duds are removed automatically. Real-time updates arrive on your phone.

03

Watch them hatch

LifeLoop notifies you when hatch day arrives. Open the chamber for the first time since you started, and greet your chicks.

Our journey

From farm problem to
provincial champion.

A short timeline of how LifeLoop went from a community-farm visit to a first-place finish at the Ontario AGrobotics finals — and what happens next.

THE BEGINNING

A real problem at a real farm

We identified this problem while visiting Open Spaces Farms, a real community farm that regularly incubates chicken eggs. We were visiting to try to identify real, difficult problems in the agricultural industry for a robotics competition we had joined (AGrobotics).

They presented us with this issue, and it stood out to us because according to the farm, there were no solutions to this problem on the market (at a non factory-farm scale).

AGROBOTICS ENTRY

LifeLoop is born

That's how LifeLoop started — it was a submission to a robotics competition. We designed a prototype of an egg incubator that can automatically do all processes internally, removing the need to open the incubator.

Egg candling (via computer vision), dud egg removal, tracking, turning, and the lockdown phase are all completed automatically and monitored through a companion app, without exposing the main chamber to outside air. This comes with the added benefit of saving farmers precious time — because all processes are automated (including many that are typically done manually) farmers do not need to interact with LifeLoop at all until the incubation cycle completes.

That's where our slogan came from: Set it. Forget it.

PROVINCIAL FINALIST

One of six teams selected

LifeLoop was selected to be a provincial finalist for AGrobotics — we were chosen to be one of six teams to present at the Western Fair District Agricultural showcase.

THE SHOWCASE

Real farmers. Real demand.

It was at this showcase that we began to see the true potential of LifeLoop. We presented the solution to real farmers, and realized that there was actual demand for a solution like this.

We were encouraged by several attending AGtech CEOs (including the judges) to take LifeLoop beyond a competition project.

MARCH · AGROBOTICS FINALS

First place · Ontario

When LifeLoop won first place across Ontario, it only solidified what we were already beginning to understand — this project had potential.

NOW · BUILDING V3

Taking it to market

Since the AGrobotics competition (which took place in March), we've been thinking this over as a team and have been doing some market validation/research. We've emailed farmers, met with startup investors for advice, and analyzed competitors on the market.

We have decided to take LifeLoop a step further, and try to turn it into an actual startup. Right now, we're in the process of designing a market ready product (LifeLoop V3). We're currently seeking funding, strategic guidance, and industry connections to bring LifeLoop V3 to market and capture this underserved segment.
